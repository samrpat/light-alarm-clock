Interface concept · 44 mm round touchscreen · touch only

Everything is one swipe from the time.

A screen this small has no room for menus, and nothing on it turns. The clock sits in the middle. Swipe to go somewhere, tap to start, hold to stop. Three gestures run the whole product.

The device is live. Use your mouse or finger on it. Shown enlarged.

Swipe up on the clock

The whole vocabulary

Three gestures. No menus, no dials, no double-taps.

Swipe

Go somewhere. Every screen is one swipe from the clock, and the opposite swipe brings you back.

Tap

Start something, or nudge a value. The top of the screen means more and the bottom means less.

Hold

For anything that could stop an alarm: turning a day off, skipping tomorrow, dismissing. The rim fills while you hold.

Changing a time

Tap above for later, below for earlier.

Nothing on the screen has to be found or dragged. The left half of every time moves the hour, the right half moves the minutes in steps of five. Hold a tap zone and it keeps going, faster the longer you hold. A change of 15 minutes is three taps. An hour is one.

The rim of a round screen is hard to hit in the dark and may be covered by the case, so no gesture depends on it.

The map

Up and down for waking. Left and right for sleeping.

Alarms live on the vertical line: the week above, tomorrow below. Sleep lives on the horizontal line: light on the left, sound on the right. Settings open with a hold on the clock. Tap any screen to open it in the device.

Two looks, one schedule

A bright face by day. Almost dark by night.

The look follows a schedule you set once, 9 PM to 7 AM to begin with. Day shows the date, a bit more light, and an accent colour you choose. Night is amber only, with no blue, dimmed through the panel, and the whole face shifts by a few pixels every minute to protect the screen. A tap lifts the night face for five seconds.

Why it works at 3 a.m.

Designed for a hand that can't see.

Targets bigger than a fingertip

A fingertip covers about 10 mm of a 44 mm screen. Every tap zone is at least 11 mm tall, and nothing depends on the rim.

Easy to snooze, hard to cancel

Any tap snoozes, up to three times. Turning an alarm off takes a deliberate hold, and the rim fills so you can see it working.

One layout everywhere

Title on top, big value in the middle, a hint underneath. Up means more, down means less. Learn one screen and you know them all.

Text that never collides

Every line sits inside a safe zone and an automatic check measures all screens in the real font before anything is built.
