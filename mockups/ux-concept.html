Interface concept · 44 mm round touchscreen

Everything is one flick from the time.

A screen this small has no room for buttons, so there aren't any. The clock sits in the middle. Swipe in any direction and you are on the thing you wanted. Four gestures run the whole product.

The device is live. Use your mouse or finger on it. Shown enlarged.

Swipe up on the clock

The whole vocabulary

Four gestures. No menus, no double-taps.

Swipe

Go somewhere. Every screen is one swipe from the clock, and the opposite swipe brings you back.

Turn

Run a finger around the edge to change a value: time, volume, brightness. Start anywhere on the rim.

Tap

On or off. The whole face is the button, so you can hit it with your eyes closed.

Hold

For the one thing that must not happen by accident: turning the alarm off.

The map

Five screens in a cross. You are never lost.

Tomorrow's alarm gets its own screen because that is the change people make most: an early flight, a late start. It applies once, then the weekly rhythm takes over again. Tap any screen to open it in the device above.

Why it works at 3 a.m.

Designed for a hand that can't see.

Targets bigger than a fingertip

A fingertip covers about 10 mm of a 44 mm screen. Most screens have one touch zone: all of it. The busiest screen, the week, has seven slices of about 14 mm each.

Dark until asked

The night face is a dim amber time and nothing else. A tap brightens it for five seconds and shows where each swipe leads. Then it settles back.

Easy to snooze, hard to cancel

Any tap snoozes, up to three times. Turning the alarm off takes a deliberate two-second hold, and the rim fills as you hold so you can see it working.

The same layout everywhere

Label on top, big value in the middle, dial on the rim. Learn one screen and you know them all.
