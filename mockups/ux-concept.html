A clock that follows your day until you sleep.

Every place has its own colour: sky for the clock, sunrise for alarms, sea for sound, gold for light. Colour belongs to the day. As bedtime nears it drains away, and the night clock is a dim amber. Drag the time slider below the device to watch it.

The whole vocabulary

Four gestures, four jobs. Nothing overlaps.

Swipe

Go to a neighbour. Up and down for waking, left and right for the next screen, day or page. The opposite swipe comes back.

Tap

Do something: play, turn on, snooze, open. The whole face is the button.

Drag

The only way to change a value. Grab a dot on the rim and slide it around, like the hands of a clock.

Hold

For anything that could stop an alarm: turning a day off, skipping tomorrow, dismissing. The rim fills while you hold.

Changing a time

Two dots, like the hands of a clock.

The outer dot is the minute hand: slide it around for five minutes a step, and the hour moves on by itself as it passes the top. The inner dot is the hour hand: one step is one hour. Only a dot responds, so a swipe anywhere else is always a swipe. In 877 random test swipes, none was mistaken for a turn. There are no buttons to find: dragging a dot is the one way to change a time.

The map

Up and down for waking. Left and right for sleeping.

Alarms live on the vertical line: the week above, tomorrow below. Sleep lives on the horizontal line: light on the left, sound on the right. Sideways always means the next neighbour: light, clock, sound; the days of the week; the settings pages. Settings open with a hold on the clock. Tap any screen to open it in the device.

A face that follows your day

Sky colours by day. Fading to amber for bed.

From your alarm onward the face is a living sky: peach at dawn, blue through the day, gold and rose toward evening. Three hours before bedtime the colour and brightness drain away on a smooth curve, and at bedtime it is the dim amber night clock. One setting drives it: your bedtime. The morning end is your alarm.

Settings

Seven settings, each a picture of what it sets.

Bedtime is a violet band of night on the clock ring, with the wind-down fading in before it. It ends at an orange bell: that is your alarm, set on the alarm screens and never here. The Alarm page has one job each for the dot and the tap: drag for volume, tap the bell to test the light and sound.

The morning light

Thirty minutes of dawn, built from the evidence.

It starts with deep red, which passes through closed eyelids and barely touches the body clock, then climbs through amber and warm white to a daylight white at the alarm. The rise is exponential, like a real dawn. After the alarm it holds bright for ten minutes. At night the ring is off completely. Press "Play a morning" to watch it.

Built to last

No Wi-Fi, no servers, no updates needed.

It never calls out

The time is set by hand once. Daylight saving is a rule stored inside the clock, with a one-tap fix if the law ever changes.

Few settings

Seven pages, and four of them you touch once: time, date, region and bedtime.

The alarm outlives the screen

If the display ever fails, the light and sound still run, and a button on the back still turns the alarm off.

Repairable

The battery is a swappable part, and the firmware and tools are kept offline so it can be rebuilt decades from now.

Concept prototype. Colours, glow and timing are simulated in the browser, and the real LED ring and AMOLED panel will look different. The date is fixed at Tuesday, Nov 12 and type is Ubuntu. Wake time is not a setting: the face treats your alarm as the start of the day.
