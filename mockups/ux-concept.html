Interface concept · 44 mm round touchscreen · touch only

A clock that follows your day until you sleep.

Few words. Swipe, tap, hold, and turn the rim. The face changes colour and brightness through the day, winds down toward bedtime, and ends as a dim amber clock. Drag the time slider below the device to watch it.

The device is live. Use your mouse or finger on it. Shown enlarged.

Drag the slider, or swipe up on the clock

The whole vocabulary

Four gestures. No menus, no double-taps.

Swipe

Go somewhere. Every screen is one swipe from the clock, and the opposite swipe brings you back.

Tap

Start something, or nudge a value. The top of the screen means more and the bottom means less.

Turn

Run a finger around the rim to set a value. Slow is one minute at a time, fast is half an hour. The marker shows where you are.

Hold

For anything that could stop an alarm: turning a day off, skipping tomorrow, dismissing. The rim fills while you hold.

Changing a time

Turn the rim, or tap above and below.

The fast way is to run a finger around the rim: clockwise is later, and the faster you go the bigger the step. The sure way is to tap: above is later, below is earlier, the left half moves the hour and the right half the minutes. If the case ever covers part of the rim, the taps still work.

The map

Up and down for waking. Left and right for sleeping.

Alarms live on the vertical line: the week above, tomorrow below. Sleep lives on the horizontal line: light on the left, sound on the right. Settings open with a hold on the clock. Tap any screen to open it in the device.

A face that follows your day

Bright and cool by day. Warm and fading toward bed.

After you wake the colour moves from gold through cool white at noon to amber. Three hours before bedtime the face winds down: the blue drains out, the brightness falls on a smooth curve, the date fades, and at bedtime it is the dim amber night clock. Two settings drive it: when you go to bed and when you wake.

The morning light

Thirty minutes of dawn, built from the evidence.

It starts with deep red, which passes through closed eyelids and barely touches the body clock, then climbs through amber and warm white to a daylight white at the alarm. The rise is exponential, like a real dawn. After the alarm it holds bright for ten minutes. At night the ring is off completely. Press "Play a morning" to watch it.

Built to last

No Wi-Fi, no servers, no updates needed.

It never calls out

The time is set by hand once. Daylight saving is a rule stored inside the clock, with a one-tap fix if the law ever changes.

Few settings

Eight pages, and four of them you touch once: time, date, region and sleep hours.

The alarm outlives the screen

If the display ever fails, the light and sound still run, and a button on the back still turns the alarm off.

Repairable

The battery is a swappable part, and the firmware and tools are kept offline so it can be rebuilt decades from now.

Concept prototype. Colours, glow and timing are simulated in the browser, and the real LED ring and AMOLED panel will look different. The date is fixed at Tuesday, Nov 12 and type is Ubuntu.
